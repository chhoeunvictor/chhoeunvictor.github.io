Vault Mobile Banking
Microcopy System

A curated set of UX copy for Vault — a fictional mobile banking app — covering error messages, empty states, success confirmations, and onboarding tooltips. Each piece is written to reduce anxiety, build trust, and keep users moving.

UX Writing Mobile · iOS & Android Fintech Spec work Victor Chhoeun
Writing intent

Financial products carry high emotional stakes — users are moving real money, making decisions that matter. Every word in a banking app needs to do two things at once: give users the information they need to act, and make them feel like the product has their back. The copy here is calm, direct, and human — never clinical, never vague.

01 · Error Messages

When things go wrong

Error messages in fintech are high-stakes. Users are already stressed. Copy needs to explain what happened, reassure them nothing bad occurred, and tell them exactly what to do next — in as few words as possible.

✕ Before

"Error: Transaction failed. Please try again later or contact support if the issue persists."

Generic, cold, and unhelpful. "Try again later" gives users no timeframe. "Contact support" feels like a dead end. No reassurance that money is safe.

✓ After

"Your transfer didn't go through — but your money is safe. Check your connection and try again. Still stuck? We're here."

Leads with reassurance, gives a clear action, and ends with a human offer of help. Conversational but not casual.

Why this works

"Didn't go through" is softer than "failed" — it implies a fixable hiccup, not a broken system. Reassuring the user their money is safe before anything else reduces panic. Two actions: self-serve first, support second.

"Take your time" slows users down before they accidentally lock themselves out. Framing lockout as "temporary" reduces alarm. The reset path is offered early — not buried after a full lockout.

"Signed out" is less alarming than "session expired" — it's familiar language. Explaining the reason (security, not a bug) reframes the interruption as something the app did on purpose to protect them.

02 · Empty States

Nothing here yet

Empty states are missed opportunities in most apps. In banking, they're a chance to reduce anxiety ("is something wrong?") and guide users toward their next action without pressure.

03 · Success Messages

When it works

Success copy in fintech needs to confirm the action clearly, give users what they need to feel confident, and get out of the way. No celebration overload — just calm, clear confirmation.

04 · Onboarding Tooltips

First-time guidance

Onboarding tooltips need to be brief, contextual, and genuinely useful. In a banking app, users are already nervous about a new product handling their money — tooltips should build confidence, not add cognitive load.

Instant transfer feature Tooltip
9:41●●●
⚡ Instant transfers
Move money between Vault accounts in seconds — any time, any day. No waiting, no fees.
Send to Vault user
Got it
Why this works

Leads with the benefit (seconds, any time, no fees) not the mechanics. Short enough to read in a glance. "Got it" dismissal is friendly — not "dismiss" or "close."

Your Vault number
This is your unique ID for receiving money on Vault. Share it like you'd share a username — it's not your account number.
@victor · #4821
Got it
Why this works

The key job is alleviating anxiety: "not your account number" directly addresses the worry users have about sharing financial identifiers. Uses familiar analogy (username) to make a new concept click.

Your insights will appear here after your first transaction.
Got it
Why this works

"The smarter it gets" sets a positive expectation without overpromising. Avoids technical language about ML or categorization algorithms — users don't need to know how it works, just what it does for them.

The biggest barrier to biometric adoption is privacy anxiety. Addressing it directly ("never leaves your device") removes the hesitation. "Not now" instead of "Skip" is less final and less negative.

01
Lead with reassurance

In fintech, users' first instinct when something goes wrong is panic. Address the emotional state before giving instructions — tell them their money is safe before explaining what to do next.

02
Be specific, not vague

Exact amounts, names, and dates build trust. "Your transfer didn't go through" is less reassuring than "$250 to Jamie L. didn't go through." Specificity signals the app knows what's happening.

03
Always give a next step

Dead-end messages lose users. Every error or empty state should tell users what they can do. Even if the answer is "contact support," frame it as an open door — not a last resort.

04
Human, not clinical

Banking copy defaults to formal language. Vault's voice is calm and direct — like a knowledgeable friend explaining something clearly, not a legal document covering its bases.
